## Measurement

### 1. Anatomical Breadth (Maximum Length)

#### Instrument Used

- Sliding caliper

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Medial (vertebral) border**
  - The midpoint of the medial border of the scapula.
- **Lateral (axillary) border at glenoid level**
  - The most lateral point on the lateral border opposite the glenoid cavity.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface with the costal surface facing downward.
2. Properly orient the bone in its anatomical position.
3. Identify the midpoint of the medial (vertebral) border.
4. Identify the most lateral point on the lateral border at the level of the glenoid cavity.
5. Open the sliding caliper and place:
   - One fixed jaw on the medial border landmark.
   - The movable jaw on the lateral border landmark.
6. Ensure the caliper jaws are parallel and just touching the bone.
7. Read the measurement in millimeters (mm).
8. Take two to three readings and record the average.

#### Precautions

- Do not include the acromion or coracoid process.
- Avoid tilting the sliding caliper.
- Apply gentle pressure only.
- Ensure accurate identification of landmarks.

### 2. Anatomical Length (Maximum Breadth)

#### Instrument Used

- Sliding caliper

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Superior angle**
  - The uppermost point at the junction of the superior and medial borders.
- **Inferior angle**
  - The lowest pointed tip of the scapula.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface in its anatomical position.
2. Ensure the bone is stable and properly oriented.
3. Identify the superior angle of the scapula.
4. Identify the inferior angle of the scapula.
5. Open the sliding caliper.
6. Place the fixed jaw of the caliper on the superior angle.
7. Place the movable jaw on the inferior angle.
8. Ensure both jaws are in a straight vertical line and just touching the bone.
9. Repeat the measurement and record the average value.

#### Precautions

- Do not measure obliquely; keep the caliper aligned vertically.
- Apply gentle pressure to avoid damage.
- Confirm correct identification of angles.

### 3. Length of Spine

#### Instrument Used

- Sliding caliper

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Medial end (root) of spine**
  - The point where the spine meets the medial (vertebral) border of the scapula.
- **Lateral end of spine (base of acromion)**
  - The point where the spine broadens and continues into the acromion process.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface with the dorsal surface facing upward.
2. Identify the root of the scapular spine on the medial border.
3. Identify the base of the acromion where the spine ends laterally.
4. Open the sliding caliper.
5. Place one jaw on the medial end (root) of the spine.
6. Place the other jaw on the lateral end of the spine at the base of the acromion.
7. Ensure the caliper is aligned along the axis of the spine.
8. Take two readings and note the average.

#### Precautions

- Do not include the acromion process itself.
- Keep the caliper straight along the spine, not oblique.
- Apply gentle pressure only.

### 4. Length of Glenoid Fossa

#### Instrument Used

- Sliding caliper

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Superior margin of glenoid fossa**
  - The uppermost point of the articular surface near the supraglenoid tubercle.
- **Inferior margin of glenoid fossa**
  - The lowermost point of the articular surface near the infraglenoid tubercle.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface with the lateral angle facing upward.
2. Identify the superior margin of the glenoid fossa.
3. Identify the inferior margin of the glenoid fossa.
4. Open the sliding caliper.
5. Place one jaw on the superior articular margin.
6. Place the other jaw on the inferior articular margin.
7. Ensure the caliper is aligned vertically (supero-inferiorly).
8. Take two readings and record the average.

#### Precautions

- Measure only the articular surface; do not include surrounding bone.
- Avoid oblique placement of the caliper.
- Apply gentle pressure to prevent damage.

### 5. Breadth of Glenoid Fossa

#### Instrument Used

- Sliding caliper

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Anterior margin of glenoid fossa**
  - The most anterior point of the articular surface.
- **Posterior margin of glenoid fossa**
  - The most posterior point of the articular surface.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface with the lateral angle facing upward.
2. Identify the anterior articular margin of the glenoid fossa.
3. Identify the posterior articular margin of the glenoid fossa.
4. Open the sliding caliper.
5. Place one jaw on the anterior margin.
6. Place the other jaw on the posterior margin.
7. Ensure the caliper is aligned antero-posteriorly.
8. Take two readings and note the average.

#### Precautions

- Measure only the articular surface, excluding the glenoid rim.
- Avoid tilted or oblique positioning of the caliper.
- Apply light pressure to prevent damage to the bone.

### 6. Supraspinous Angle

#### Instrument Used

- Goniometer

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Spine of scapula**
  - The long axis of the scapular spine.
- **Superior border of scapula**
  - The upper border extending from the superior angle toward the coracoid region.
- **Point of intersection**
  - The root of the spine, where the spine meets the medial border.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface with the dorsal surface facing upward.
2. Identify the root of the scapular spine.
3. Align one arm of the goniometer along the spine of the scapula.
4. Align the other arm of the goniometer along the superior border of the scapula.
5. Ensure both arms meet at the root of the spine.
6. Read the angle directly from the goniometer scale.
7. Record the measurement in degrees (°).
8. Repeat and take the average value.

#### Precautions

- Ensure correct identification of the root of the spine.
- Keep the goniometer arms accurately aligned with the borders.
- Avoid movement of the bone during measurement.

### 7. Infraspinous Angle

#### Instrument Used

- Goniometer

#### Anatomical Landmarks

- **Spine of scapula**
  - The long axis of the scapular spine.
- **Lateral (axillary) border**
  - The outer border of the scapula extending toward the inferior angle.
- **Point of intersection**
  - The root of the spine, where the spine meets the medial border.

#### Procedure

1. Place the scapula on a flat surface with the dorsal surface facing upward.
2. Identify the root of the scapular spine.
3. Align one arm of the goniometer along the spine of the scapula.
4. Align the other arm along the lateral (axillary) border.
5. Ensure both arms meet precisely at the root of the spine.
6. Read the angle from the goniometer scale.
7. Record the measurement in degrees (°).
8. Repeat the measurement and take the average.

#### Precautions

- Confirm accurate identification of the lateral border.
- Keep the goniometer steady and properly aligned.
- Avoid parallax error while reading the scale.
