MEASUREMENT:

	•	 ANATOMICAL BREADTH (MAXIMUM LENGTH)

Instrument Used: Sliding caliper
Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Medial (Vertebral) Border
	•	The midpoint of the medial border of the scapula.
	•	Lateral (Axillary) Border at Glenoid Level
	•	The most lateral point on the lateral border opposite the glenoid cavity.


PROCEDURE

	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface with the costal surface facing downward.
	•	Properly orient the bone in its anatomical position.
	•	Identify the midpoint of the medial (vertebral) border.
	•	Identify the most lateral point on the lateral border at the level of the glenoid cavity.
	•	Open the sliding caliper and place:
	•	One fixed jaw on the medial border landmark
	•	The movable jaw on the lateral border landmark
	•	Ensure the caliper jaws are parallel and just touching the bone.
	•	Read the measurement in millimeters (mm).
	•	Take two to three readings and record the average.


PRECAUTIONS:
	•	Do not include the acromion or coracoid process.
	•	Avoid tilting the sliding caliper.
	•	Apply gentle pressure only.
	•	Ensure accurate identification of landmarks.


	•	ANATOMICAL LENGTH (MAXIMUM BREADTH)

Instrument used: Sliding caliper

Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Superior Angle
	•	The uppermost point at the junction of the superior and medial borders.
	•	Inferior Angle
	•	The lowest pointed tip of the scapula.

PROCEDURE
	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface in its anatomical position.
	•	Ensure the bone is stable and properly oriented.
	•	Identify the superior angle of the scapula.
	•	Identify the inferior angle of the scapula.
	•	Open the sliding caliper.
	•	Place the fixed jaw of the caliper on the superior angle.
	•	Place the movable jaw on the inferior angle.
	•	Ensure both jaws are in a straight vertical line and just touching the bone.
	•	Repeat the measurement and record the average value.


      PRECAUTIONS
	•	Do not measure obliquely; keep the caliper aligned vertically.
	•	Apply gentle pressure to avoid damage.
	•	Confirm correct identification of angles.


	•	LENGTH OF SPINE 

Instrument Used: Sliding caliper

Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Medial End (Root) of Spine
	•	The point where the spine meets the medial (vertebral) border of the scapula.
	•	Lateral End of Spine (Base of Acromion)
	•	The point where the spine broadens and continues into the acromion process.

PROCEDURE
	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface with the dorsal surface facing upward.
	•	Identify the root of the scapular spine on the medial border.
	•	Identify the base of the acromion where the spine ends laterally.
	•	Open the sliding caliper.
	•	Place one jaw on the medial end (root) of the spine.
	•	Place the other jaw on the lateral end of the spine at the base of the acromion.
	•	Ensure the caliper is aligned along the axis of the spine.
	•	Take two readings and note the average.


PRECAUTIONS
	•	Do not include the acromion process itself.
	•	Keep the caliper straight along the spine, not oblique.
	•	Apply gentle pressure only.
	•	LENGTH OF GLENOID FOSSA

Instrument Used: Sliding caliper

Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Superior Margin of Glenoid Fossa
	•	The uppermost point of the articular surface near the supraglenoid tubercle.
	•	Inferior Margin of Glenoid Fossa
	•	The lowermost point of the articular surface near the infraglenoid tubercle.

PROCEDURE
	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface with the lateral angle facing upward.
	•	Identify the superior margin of the glenoid fossa.
	•	Identify the inferior margin of the glenoid fossa.
	•	Open the sliding caliper.
	•	Place one jaw on the superior articular margin.
	•	Place the other jaw on the inferior articular margin.
	•	Ensure the caliper is aligned vertically (supero-inferiorly).
	•	Take two readings and record the average.


PRECAUTIONS
	•	Measure only the articular surface; do not include surrounding bone.
	•	Avoid oblique placement of the caliper.
	•	Apply gentle pressure to prevent damage.



	•	BREADTH OF GLENOID FOSSA

Instrument Used: Sliding caliper

Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Anterior Margin of Glenoid Fossa
	•	The most anterior point of the articular surface.
	•	Posterior Margin of Glenoid Fossa
	•	The most posterior point of the articular surface.


PROCEDURE
	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface with the lateral angle facing upward.
	•	Identify the anterior articular margin of the glenoid fossa.
	•	Identify the posterior articular margin of the glenoid fossa.
	•	Open the sliding caliper.
	•	Place one jaw on the anterior margin.
	•	Place the other jaw on the posterior margin.
	•	Ensure the caliper is aligned antero-posteriorly.
	•	Take two readings and note the average.

PRECAUTIONS
	•	Measure only the articular surface, excluding the glenoid rim.
	•	Avoid tilted or oblique positioning of the caliper.
	•	Apply light pressure to prevent damage to the bone.


	•	SUPRA- SPINOUS ANGLE

Instrument Used: Goniometer

Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Spine of Scapula
	•	The long axis of the scapular spine.
	•	Superior Border of Scapula
	•	The upper border extending from the superior angle toward the coracoid region.
	•	Point of Intersection
	•	The root of the spine, where the spine meets the medial border.


PROCEDURE
	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface with the dorsal surface facing upward.
	•	Identify the root of the scapular spine.
	•	Align one arm of the goniometer along the spine of the scapula.
	•	Align the other arm of the goniometer along the superior border of the scapula.
	•	Ensure both arms meet at the root of the spine.
	•	Read the angle directly from the goniometer scale.
	•	Record the measurement in degrees (°).
	•	Repeat and take the average value.


PRECAUTIONS
	•	Ensure correct identification of the root of the spine.
	•	Keep the goniometer arms accurately aligned with the borders.
	•	Avoid movement of the bone during measurement.



	•	INFRA – SPINOUS ANGLE

Instrument Used: Goniometer
Anatomical Landmarks
	•	Spine of Scapula
	•	The long axis of the scapular spine.
	•	Lateral (Axillary) Border
	•	The outer border of the scapula extending toward the inferior angle.
	•	Point of Intersection
	•	The root of the spine, where the spine meets the medial border.


PROCEDURE
	•	Place the scapula on a flat surface with the dorsal surface facing upward.
	•	Identify the root of the scapular spine.
	•	Align one arm of the goniometer along the spine of the scapula.
	•	Align the other arm along the lateral (axillary) border.
	•	Ensure both arms meet precisely at the root of the spine.
	•	Read the angle from the goniometer scale.
	•	Record the measurement in degrees (°).
	•	Repeat the measurement and take the average.


PRECAUTIONS
	•	Confirm accurate identification of the lateral border.
	•	Keep the goniometer steady and properly aligned.
	•	Avoid parallax error while reading the scale.
